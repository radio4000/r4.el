* r4.el
Browse, play and download [[https://radio4000.com][radio4000.com]] channels from Emacs: everything
radio4000.com and the =r4= command line do, with the same words, in
Emacs lists and menus.

- A home buffer, channel pages, and lists of channels and tracks, with
  menus like magit: =M-x r4=.
- Browse, explore and search channels and tracks, with the search
  language of radio4000.com: =@ko002 #jazz miles=.
- Play with =mpv=: a queue, shuffle, play next, history, videos in
  Emacs, and the current track in the mode line.
- Auto-radio: the same live radio as radio4000.com, where everyone
  hears the same track at the same second; and live broadcasts, to
  listen to or to broadcast your player.
- Download tracks as the best audio, or videos, with =yt-dlp=, into the
  same folders as the =r4= command line, with metadata.  Downloaded
  tracks play from disk.
- Follow channels, read your feed, and edit your channels and tracks.
- Local-first channels, without an account, synced later to
  radio4000.com.

See [[file:features.org][features.org]] for what radio4000.com and the command line do, and
how to do it in Emacs.

** Requirements
- Emacs 29.1 or newer.
- [[https://mpv.io][mpv]], to play tracks.
- [[https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp][yt-dlp]] (recent), and =ffmpeg=, to download and stream tracks.

YouTube changes often: keep yt-dlp up to date (=pipx install yt-dlp=
or =pipx upgrade yt-dlp=, distribution packages are often too old).
yt-dlp also needs a JavaScript runtime for YouTube, see [[https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/EJS][yt-dlp/wiki/EJS]].
With =deno= installed it works out of the box; for =bun= or =node=,
add it to =~/.config/yt-dlp/config= (used by both r4 downloads and
mpv streaming):

#+begin_src conf
--js-runtimes bun
#+end_src

Give its full path when Emacs, started from your desktop, does not have
it in its =PATH=, such as =--js-runtimes bun:/home/you/.bun/bin/bun=.

When YouTube asks to "sign in to confirm you're not a bot", often after
many downloads, pass the cookies of your browser to yt-dlp, in the
same config file or in =r4-download-arguments=:

#+begin_src conf
--cookies-from-browser firefox
#+end_src

** Installation
r4.el is installed from its git repository with =package-vc=, built
into Emacs 29.1 and newer. It installs the =.el= files of the default
branch, byte-compiles them and generates their autoloads.

*** Emacs 30 and newer, with use-package
=use-package= installs the package on first start with its =:vc= keyword:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(use-package r4
  :vc (:url "https://github.com/radio4000/r4.el" :rev :newest)
  :bind ("C-c r" . r4)
  :bind-keymap ("C-c 4" . r4-command-map)
  :custom
  (r4-download-directory "~/Music/radio4000/"))
#+end_src

=:rev :newest= installs the latest commit. Without it, use-package
installs the latest release: the last commit that changed the
=Version:= header of =r4.el=.

*** Emacs 29
=use-package= in Emacs 29 has no =:vc= keyword: install the package
once, then configure it with =use-package=:

#+begin_example
M-x package-vc-install RET https://github.com/radio4000/r4.el RET
#+end_example

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(use-package r4
  :bind ("C-c r" . r4)
  :bind-keymap ("C-c 4" . r4-command-map)
  :custom
  (r4-download-directory "~/Music/radio4000/"))
#+end_src

*** Update and remove
- =M-x package-vc-upgrade RET r4= pulls the latest commits and
  rebuilds the package (=package-vc-upgrade-all= updates all of them).
  Restart Emacs after an upgrade: the version loaded before may linger
  in open r4 buffers.
- =M-x package-delete RET r4= removes it.

*** From a local clone, for development
#+begin_example
git clone git@github.com:radio4000/r4.el.git
M-x package-vc-install-from-checkout RET ~/path/to/r4.el RET r4 RET
#+end_example

Changes in the clone are then used directly, after =M-x
package-vc-rebuild=, or by evaluating the edited buffers.

** Usage
=M-x r4= opens the home buffer, like the home page of radio4000.com:
the player and its controls, your channels, the synced and local
channels, live broadcasts and what you played lately.  Start there, or
with these commands:

| Command               | Description                                         |
|-----------------------+-----------------------------------------------------|
| =r4=                  | The home buffer                                     |
| =r4-channels=         | List channels, most recently active first           |
| =r4-explore=          | Featured channels, with artwork, with many tracks…  |
| =r4-channel-page=     | Visit a channel, like /@slug                        |
| =r4-search=           | Search tracks: =@channel #tag words=                |
| =r4-play-channel=     | Play a channel                                      |
| =r4-auto-radio=       | Tune in to the auto-radio of a channel              |
| =r4-broadcasts=       | Live broadcasts, RET to listen                      |
| =r4-feed=             | Latest tracks of the channels you follow            |
| =r4-history=          | The tracks you played                               |
| =r4-download-channel= | Download a channel                                  |
| =r4-menu=             | The menu of all the commands                        |

*** From anywhere
r4 binds no global keys: choose yours, as in the examples above.

- =r4= opens the home buffer, where the player keys work; =C-c r= in
  the examples.
- =r4-command-map= holds the player keys from any buffer, under the
  prefix you choose, =C-c 4= in the examples: =C-c 4 SPC= pauses,
  =C-c 4 n= and =C-c 4 p= play the next and previous track, =C-c 4 f=
  and =C-c 4 b= seek, =C-c 4 v= shows the player window, =C-c 4 w=
  shares the playing track, =C-c 4 l= goes live, =C-c 4 j= visits a
  channel, =C-c 4 s= searches, and =C-c 4 ?= opens the menu of all the
  commands.

*** Menus and keys
Like in magit, =?= opens the menu of the buffer you are in: its heading
says what it acts on, such as the list and the track at point, and its
keys work without opening it.  In the home buffer, =?= opens the menu
of all the commands, =r4-menu=.

The main views are a key away in every r4 buffer, and in each menu.
Like in Dired, =^= goes up: from the tracks of a channel to its page,
to the channels, to home.

| Key           | Go to                          |
|---------------+--------------------------------|
| =^=           | Up, to home                    |
| =j=           | Visit a channel                |
| =E= =B=       | Explore, broadcasts            |
| =H=           | History                        |

*** The player
Tracks play with mpv, from disk when downloaded.  The player keys work
in every r4 buffer.  They are on Meta, and leave the keys of Emacs
alone: =n= and =p= move between lines, =M-n= and =M-p= between tracks.

| Key             | Player                         |
|-----------------+--------------------------------|
| =SPC=           | Pause, resume                  |
| =M-n= =M-p=     | Next, previous track           |
| =M-f= =M-b=     | Seek forward, backward         |
| =M-<up>= =M-<down>= | Volume                     |
| =Z=             | Toggle shuffle                 |
| =V=             | Toggle video, =C-u V= pops it out |
| =Q=           | Queue, where =/= =#= filter what plays |
| =v=           | Player window                  |
| =;=           | Share the playing track        |
| =b=           | Go live: broadcast, or stop    |
| =k=           | Stop                           |

While playing, the mode line shows =r4 ▶ Title 1:23/4:56=
(=r4-player-mode-line-mode=; set =r4-player-mode-line= to nil to hide
it): click it to pause, middle-click for the next track, right-click
for the queue.  While downloading, it shows =r4 ↓12=, the downloads to
do: click it to list them.

=v= shows the player in a compact window at the top of the frame: the
track, its time, and a progress bar to click to seek.  =V= shows the
video there too, beside the player in a wide window, under it in a
narrow one; =C-u V= pops it out in a window of mpv, and back
(=r4-player-video-pop-out=).  Emacs built for Wayland only (pgtk) and
macOS show videos in a window of mpv.

=b= broadcasts what you play as your channel, like the live button of
radio4000.com: listeners hear the same tracks at the same position, on
radio4000.com or in Emacs with =B=.  The mode line then shows =live=,
and =b= again stops.

*** Lists
Lists of channels and tracks are =tabulated-list-mode= buffers: =n= =p=
move, =g= refreshes, =q= quits.  Tracks are marked like files in
Dired: =m= marks, =u= unmarks, =U= unmarks all, =t= toggles the marks,
=% m= marks by regexp, and actions apply to the marked tracks, or to
the one at point.  =S= sorts by the column at
point, or click its header; dates and counts sort newest and largest
first, =S= again reverses.

In lists of tracks:

| Key           | Tracks                                                   |
|---------------+----------------------------------------------------------|
| =RET=         | Play from this track                                     |
| =P= =z= =R=   | Play all, shuffle, auto-radio                            |
| =a= =N=       | Add to the queue, play next                              |
| =d= =D=       | Download the marked tracks (or at point), all            |
| =s=           | Search: =#tag words=, in this channel                     |
| =/= =#=       | Filter the shown tracks, by text or toggling tags        |
| =i= =@=       | Describe the track, visit its channel page               |
| =A= =e= =x=   | Add, edit, delete tracks (your channel or a local one)   |
| =c= =C= =Y=   | Copy to a local channel, save locally, sync              |
| =o= =O=       | Open on radio4000.com, open the media                    |
| =w= =W=       | Copy the media URL, the radio4000.com URL                |
| =F= =L=       | Visit the downloaded file, list downloads                |
| =y=           | Sync the channel locally, or stop                        |

In lists of channels:

| Key           | Channels                                                 |
|---------------+----------------------------------------------------------|
| =RET=         | Visit the channel page, like /@slug                      |
| =T=           | Open the tracks                                          |
| =P= =z= =R=   | Play, shuffle, auto-radio                                |
| =f= =F= =r=   | Follow or unfollow, its following, its followers         |
| =M=           | Tracks mentioning it                                     |
| =D= =C= =y=   | Download, save locally, sync locally                     |
| =s= =/= =t=   | Search channels, filter the shown channels, search tracks |
| =A= =e= =x=   | Create, edit, delete your channels                       |
| =o= =w=       | Open on radio4000.com, copy its URL                      |

The status column of track lists, the history and the feed shows the
file of each track, =✓= downloaded, =↓= downloading, =✗= failed, and the
player, =▶= playing, =·= played before.  The status column of channel
lists shows =▶= the channel playing, =♥= the channels your channel
follows, =⟳= the synced channels, and =↓= while downloading or =✓= once
downloaded.

Click a tag of a track to filter the list by it, like on
radio4000.com, and click it in the mode line to remove it; =#= toggles
tags too, with their counts.  Searches match whole words, like on
radio4000.com, and also the beginning of words: =osk= finds =oskar=.
=@slug= targets a channel and =#tag= a tag; use quotes, =or= and =-= for
a web search, such as =jazz -blues=.

Bookmark any list with =C-x r m=, such as a search or a filtered
channel, to open it again with =C-x r b=: the saved views of
radio4000.com.  With [[https://github.com/oantolin/embark][Embark]], act on the channels of any completion.

*** Channel pages
=RET= on a channel, =j= anywhere, or =@= on a track visits the page of
a channel, laid out like =describe-package=: its image, website,
tracks, location, links and description, where =#tags= and =@mentions=
are buttons, its most used tags, the actions and their keys, and its
latest tracks.

** Downloads
=d=, =D= and =r4-download-channel= download the best audio available,
without re-encoding: often Opus or AAC from YouTube.  They use the same
folders and file names as =r4 download=, so the command line, Emacs
and the radio4000.com import share them:

#+begin_example
~/Music/radio4000/ko002/
├── ko002.txt          the channel
├── ko002.json         a local copy of the channel, if saved
├── ko002.m3u          playlist of the downloaded files
├── tracks.m3u         playlist of the track URLs
├── image.url          the channel image
├── failures.jsonl     the downloads that failed
└── tracks/
    ├── Artist - Title [youtube-id].opus
    └── Artist - Title [youtube-id].txt
#+end_example

Files get the artist, title, album (the channel), description and
comment (the radio4000.com URL) of the track, and the date it was added
to the channel.  Failed downloads are skipped next time, like the
command line: retry them with =C-u d=, or =R= in =r4-downloads=.

Set =r4-download-video= to download videos instead, in
=r4-download-video-quality= and =r4-download-video-container=; audio and
video files of a track live side by side.  =V= plays videos, see The
player above.

Downloads of r4.el 0.2 were outside =tracks/=: =M-x r4-files-migrate=
moves them.

** Synced channels
Choose the channels to keep synced locally, such as a few of the ones
you follow: =y= in a channel list, or in the list of a channel, syncs
it or stops syncing it.  Their new tracks download:
- with =M-x r4-sync-channels=, or =y= in the =r4-menu=;
- when you open an r4 list, at most every =r4-sync-interval= (an hour);
- while they play, to not stream them again (=r4-player-download=,
  =t= for all tracks, =nil= for none).

=r4-list-synced-channels= lists them, and =r4-synced-channels= holds
their slugs, saved with Customize.

** Auto-radio and broadcasts
=R= tunes in to the auto-radio of a channel, like the infinity button
of radio4000.com: everyone listening hears the same track at the same
second, without a broadcaster.  =r4-broadcasts= (=B=) lists the
channels broadcasting live; =RET= follows one, until you play something
else.  =b= broadcasts your own player, see [[*The player][The player]].

** Configuration
=M-x customize-group RET r4=. For example:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq r4-download-directory "~/Music/radio4000/" ; the default
      r4-download-audio-format "flac"            ; nil keeps the original audio, without re-encoding
      r4-download-max-processes 3
      r4-channels-min-tracks 10)                 ; hide small channels
#+end_src

Choose the columns of the lists, named after the fields of the
channels and tracks of radio4000.com, and sort them with =S= on a
column or by clicking its header; dates and counts sort newest and
largest first, =S= again reverses:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq r4-channels-columns
      '(status slug name track_count latest_track_at created_at updated_at description)
      r4-tracks-columns
      '(status title duration tags slug created_at updated_at description))
#+end_src

The other columns are =url= and =image= for channels, and =provider=,
=media_id=, =url= and =discogs_url= for tracks.

The colors come from the faces of r4, which inherit those of your
theme: =M-x customize-group RET r4-faces=.

** Local channels
A local channel is a file in the radio4000.com backup format
(version 2), =~/Music/radio4000/SLUG/SLUG.json=, next to its downloads.
=r4-local-create-channel= creates one, =c= copies tracks to it from any
list, and =C= saves a copy of a channel to browse it offline.

A local channel is only on your computer: its tracks have no
radio4000.com links, =s= searches the channel itself, and its menu
offers to sync it instead, with =Y=.

It can be imported into radio4000.com, versioned with git, or synced
between machines like any other file.

** Account and sync
Signed in, you can follow channels (=f=), read your feed (=r4-feed=),
and add, edit and delete your channels and tracks (=A=, =e=, =x=).
Set =r4-my-channel= if your account has several channels.

=r4-login= signs in with a code sent by email, like radio4000.com, or
with a password. A password stored in =auth-source= is used without
asking, for example in =~/.authinfo.gpg=:

#+begin_example
machine radio4000.com login you@example.org password secret
#+end_example

The session is saved in =r4-session.eld= in =user-emacs-directory=
(readable only by you, see =r4-auth-session-file=) and refreshed
automatically.

=r4-sync-channel= (or =Y= in a local channel) then:
- publishes the local channel if it is not on radio4000.com yet, or
  links it to your radio4000.com channel with the same slug;
- uploads the tracks only in the local channel;
- downloads the tracks only on radio4000.com.

A sync never deletes tracks: remove a track on both sides to delete
it. Local and remote channels and tracks share the same ids, so
syncing again is safe. To work on an existing channel of yours, start
with =r4-sync-pull-channel=.
